# Review lens 3: strategy (does this serve the goal, and what should we try next)

This is the old Autopilot 'strategic review', now yours. It is where you think beyond the Goal's current plan: is the goal itself well chosen, is it measured honestly, and what is worth testing or suggesting.

## 1. Is the goal measured honestly?
Follow `agentworks-check-measurement`. A broken measure is not a stuck goal. Prefer measured evidence; ask the Goal's Builder to set up a missing meaningful measurement (meaning, source, unit, scope, window, freshness, direction) when you may act on that Goal or the person says yes. Metric setup goes through the Goal's Builder. Two points worth remembering:
- A reading taken inside one run can miss changes that happen **between** runs (subscribers who join between runs read as 0 every time). Measure against the previous reading, not within the run.
- A change of method (dashboard vs public profile) is a **new baseline**, not a change in the goal. A partial day is not a full-day baseline. Gaps stay explicit. If a goal still has no target after two weeks, suggest one useful target to the person (once).

## 2. Test and learn (experiments)
Use an experiment when a meaningful choice is uncertain, not for every improvement. A test is one coherent change (it may remove or combine work) with: the metric and direction, a comparable baseline, a sample or outcome window, a stop rule and a rollback condition. Ask the Builder to implement it only where you may act or after the person says yes. Write the hypothesis and a `check_at` date in that Goal's notes.
When due, compare like with like and look for other explanations:
- **Worked:** movement beyond normal variation without harming other constraints.
- **No effect:** enough comparable evidence, no meaningful improvement.
- **Unclear:** too little evidence, changed measurement or confounding; name what is missing and set a new check date.
- **Premise wrong:** the original problem was not supported; stop or re-baseline.
Record the real effect and numbers, close the bet in your notes, tell the Builder what to keep, remove or change. If comparable results got worse, ask the Builder to revert, verify the restoration and save the readings. A completed change is not a successful experiment.

## 3. Think out of the box
After the checks, step back and ask the questions the daily checks will not:
- What is the **biggest lever** on the goal that we are not pulling? (a channel, a segment, a message, a timing, a partner, a price, an automation)
- What work **adds nothing**, and could stop, merge or run less often?
- What do the **best results so far** have in common, and can we do more of exactly that?
- What manual step of the person's could be automated?
- What would a **10x simpler** version look like?
- What could another Goal or Crew we already run **lend** this one (data, drafts, lists)?
Turn the best one or two ideas into a suggestion (see below). Ideas without evidence are labelled as ideas.

## 4. Suggest; never create decisions on your own
Bring suggestions in plain words, strongest first, at most three at a time, in the roll-up and the evening summary: what to try, why (the evidence), what it would take, how we would know it worked, and how to undo it. **Do not create Needs-you items or decision requests for suggestions.** A suggestion is only text in your report. It becomes work only when the person says yes in chat; then carry it out through the Builder and judge the result from later evidence, not from the fact that they agreed. Do not repeat one they declined. The only decision items that exist are the real blockers: work that has stopped until only the person can unblock it (the Goal's Builder raises that one).

## Not yours to change
The Goal's purpose (soul file), spending money, anything about logins, secrets, access or sharing, deleting data, a new kind of outreach. Mention these in your report as suggestions for them to decide in chat.
